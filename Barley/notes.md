0625:
Field observations: June 25: I observed the single 90-ft barley row. Overall plant height ranged from approximately 15 to 28 inches. The two-row barley appeared shorter overall and more mature, whereas the six-row barley was generally taller and remained mostly green to blue-green.

Maturity was uneven across the row, but the trend was clear: the two-row barley was further advanced, with some heads turning yellow to golden and one plant already fully yellow, dry, and hard. In contrast, the six-row barley was generally less mature, with most heads still green or blue-green and showing little obvious yellowing.

Kernel firmness also varied with maturity. The fully yellow two-row plant felt dry and hard, whereas greener heads were still relatively soft. Grain firmness will continue to be monitored over the next several days, particularly in the more mature two-row plants, to avoid missing the optimal seed harvest window.

0702:
Barley field observation:About one-third of the two-row barley has started turning yellow. Most of the two-row barley heads are yellow-green, with more yellow than green, with some plants fully yellow. However, the kernels are still slightly soft and can still be dented with a fingernail, so they are not fully mature yet.

Most of the six-row barley** is still green, with some yellowing beginning on the heads and awns. Overall, the six-row barley appears less mature than the two-row barley.

The cooler weather may have slowed maturity, as the highest temperature over the past week was around 70°F.

0713:
The barley appeared shorter than expected under non-irrigated conditions. Many standing heads contained soft, partially filled, or unfilled kernels, suggesting incomplete grain filling, likely associated with water limitation during heading and grain development. Some mature stems and heads also broke or lodged, causing additional grain loss.
